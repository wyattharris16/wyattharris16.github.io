What the Numbers Say

Utah's housing market is in a slow, steady phase. The statewide median sale price was $530,000 in September 2026, up 1.9% from a year earlier, while closed sales fell 11.9%. Prices are holding, but fewer homes are trading.

The Kem C. Gardner Policy Institute's 2025–2026 State of the State's Housing Market report found that Utah's median sale price across all housing types rose from $500,000 to $520,000 between the first quarters of 2025 and 2026, and that Utah ranks as the nation's 10th most expensive single-family market.

For investors, the key fact is that rents have not kept up with prices. Utah's statewide median asking rent of $2,350 against an average home value of about $509,500 works out to a gross rental yield of only about 5.6%, which leaves thin cap rates once expenses come out.

My investor takeaways
  • Underwrite on today's rent, not on appreciation.
  • More inventory and longer days on market mean room to negotiate.
  • Small multifamily and accessory dwelling units help the math work.
  • Ogden and St. George show the highest gross yields of the metros I track.
