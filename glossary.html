Investing Glossary

Plain-English definitions of the real estate terms used across this site.

A B C D E G H I L M N O P S U V #

Accessory Dwelling Unit (ADU)
A second, self-contained living unit on a single-family lot, such as a basement apartment. Utah law makes many internal ADUs legal, and they can turn a break-even home into a cash-flowing one.
After-Repair Value (ARV)
What a property should be worth once renovations are complete. Flips and BRRRR deals are built around it.
Amortization
Paying off a loan over time with regular payments. Early payments are mostly interest; later payments are mostly principal.
Appraisal
An independent opinion of a property's market value, usually built from comparable sales. Lenders require one before funding a loan.
BRRRR
Buy, Rehab, Rent, Refinance, Repeat. A strategy where you add value, then refinance to pull your cash back out for the next deal.
Cap Rate
Capitalization rate: NOI divided by price. It measures a property's unlevered return and is how investors compare income properties.
Cap Rate Compression
When market cap rates fall, values rise even if NOI doesn't change. The reverse, expansion, lowers values.
Capital Expenditures (CapEx)
Big, infrequent costs like roofs, HVAC systems and water heaters. Smart investors set aside a monthly reserve for them.
Cash Flow
What's left each month after all operating expenses and the mortgage payment.
Cash-on-Cash Return
Annual cash flow divided by the cash you actually invested, including down payment and closing costs.
Comparable Sales (Comps)
Recent sales of similar nearby properties, adjusted for differences, used to estimate value.
Days on Market (DOM)
How long a listing takes to go under contract. Rising DOM usually means buyers are gaining leverage.
Debt Service
The total loan payments (principal and interest) on a property.
DSCR
Debt service coverage ratio: NOI divided by annual debt service. Lenders typically want 1.20x to 1.25x or more.
Effective Gross Income (EGI)
Gross potential rent plus other income, minus vacancy and credit loss.
Equity Multiple
Total cash returned divided by total cash invested. A 2.0x multiple means you doubled your money.
Gross Potential Rent (GPR)
Total rent if every unit were leased all year at market rent.
Gross Rent Multiplier (GRM)
Price divided by annual gross rent. A lower number generally means a better value.
Gross Rental Yield
Annual rent divided by property value, before expenses. Utah's statewide figure is around 5.6%.
Highest and Best Use
The legal, physically possible and financially feasible use that produces the highest value for a property.
House Hacking
Living in one unit of a property and renting out the others (or a basement) to cover the mortgage. Popular with BYU and UVU students.
IRR
Internal rate of return: the annualized return that accounts for the timing of every cash flow over the hold period.
Leverage
Using borrowed money to buy property. It magnifies both gains and losses.
LTV
Loan-to-value: the loan amount divided by the property's value.
MLS
Multiple Listing Service: the database agents use to list and search properties. In most of Utah it's UtahRealEstate.com.
Months of Supply
How long current inventory would last at the current sales pace. Under about 4 months favors sellers; over 6 favors buyers.
NOI
Net operating income: effective gross income minus operating expenses. It never includes mortgage payments, depreciation or income taxes.
Operating Expenses
The costs of running a property: taxes, insurance, repairs, management, utilities the owner pays, HOA dues and reserves.
PITI
Principal, interest, taxes and insurance: the full monthly housing payment.
PMI
Private mortgage insurance, usually required when a buyer puts less than 20% down on a conventional loan.
Pro Forma
A projection of a property's future income and expenses. Seller pro formas are often optimistic, so rebuild them yourself.
Sale-to-List Ratio
Sale price divided by list price. Above 100% means homes are selling over asking.
Underwriting
Analyzing a deal's income, expenses, financing and risks to decide whether, and at what price, to buy.
Vacancy Rate
The share of potential rent lost to empty units. Most investors budget 5% to 8%.
Value-Add
Buying a property and increasing its NOI through renovations, better management or new income sources.
1% Rule
A quick screen that says monthly rent should be at least 1% of the purchase price. Very few Utah single-family homes pass it today, which is why investors here lean on other metrics.
70% Rule
A flipper's rule of thumb: pay no more than 70% of the after-repair value, minus the rehab cost.
